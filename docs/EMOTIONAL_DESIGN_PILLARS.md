# Emotional design pillars — director-approved intent

## The feeling to preserve
The original social farm game inspired anticipation before a crop matured, delight upon returning to harvest, excitement about special event seeds, and pride from patiently saving in-game currency for a desired item.

The point is not maximal production throughput. It is attachment and meaning.

## Rules
1. **Small, meaningful planting capacity.** Preserve the importance of individual plots. Avoid hundreds of low-significance fields and industrial scaling as default progression. Exact initial/max plot counts remain pending historical inventory and balance.
2. **Each crop matters.** Strong silhouette, beautiful growth stages, satisfying mature state, and distinct visual treatment. A rare/event crop should feel visibly exceptional.
3. **Anticipation without coercion.** Real-world growth waits create pleasant return moments, but no mandatory constant checking, harsh withering, or artificially painful timers to drive premium spending.
4. **Earned purchases.** Save coins over days for memorable goals: new land, desirable animals, house/farm decorations and other progression milestones. Meaningful savings should not equal predatory scarcity.
5. **Seasonal wonder.** Limited-time seeds and events should inspire discovery and sharing; exclusive cosmetic memories should not generate permanent competitive imbalance.
6. **Social emotion.** Visits, helping, playful theft and guardian reactions create small stories, not frustration or harassment.
7. **Polish matters.** Music, sounds, crop readiness animation, item purchase celebration, and atmosphere reinforce emotional attachment.
8. **Protect original essence before reinventing.** Prioritize historical mechanics catalog and faithful functional reconstruction, then modern upgrades.

## Guardrails for ongoing G1.3 economy
- Do not scale coins/land such that early aspiration becomes trivial.
- Avoid one dominant crop strategy that makes every other crop irrelevant.
- Consider meaningful medium-term savings targets and return rhythms.
- Track unique crop identity and rarity separately from raw yield.
- Keep test-time acceleration isolated from real gameplay.
- Model free player progression before monetization tuning.

## Open design questions (not blockers today)
- Historically grounded starting and maximum plot counts.
- Historical unlock ladder and crops/event catalog.
- Which long-term saving goals are most iconic and can be reinterpreted originally.
- How nostalgia-driven scarcity should adapt to current PC/mobile habits.
